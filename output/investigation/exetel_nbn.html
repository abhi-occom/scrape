Network Termination Device

NTD stands for Network Termination Device—basically, the box that connects your nbn™ service to the network. It receives the internet signal and sends it on to your modem or router. Without the NTD, you won’t be able to get online! This connection box may look a bit different dependent on the technology available at your address and the device requires power to operate. Please note: This device is separate to your modem. 

Typical Evening Speeds Explained

Typical Evening Speed (TES) is the average speed experienced by a representative group of users between the hours of 7pm and 11pm. It is not a guaranteed minimum speed and past performance is not an indication of expected future speed. The actual speed you will experience depends on many factors, like your access technology, the total demand on the network, local internet traffic, the performance of your wireless network and devices and your physical line condition. For more information see: about nbn™ speeds.

What can affect my nbn Internet Speed?

A few things can slow your nbn speed down: 

  • Connection type: Different nbn setups (FTTP, FTTN, FTTC, etc.) deliver different speeds. 

  • Network congestion: Slower speeds can happen when lots of people are using the internet at once. 

  • Wi-Fi issues: If you’re on Wi-Fi, things like distance, walls, and interference can impact speed. 

  • Device limitations: Older devices might not support higher speeds. 

  • Equipment: Faulty or outdated gear (like your modem or cables) can also cause slowdowns.

What is CGNAT?

Carrier-Grade Network Address Translation is a method of sharing a single unique public Internet Protocol (IP) address with multiple customer internet services. This allows internet providers to extend their pool of remaining Internet Protocol Version 4 (IPv4) addresses. 

Why is Exetel changing to CGNAT?

Globally, the pool of available IPv4 addresses will soon be exhausted, so Exetel has chosen to use CGNAT as a way to share the limited supply of public IPv4 addresses ahead of full deployment of IPv6. All Exetel One plans come with a dynamic IPv6 address at no extra cost. It’s great for things like remote access, hosting servers, and online gaming.

Do I need a new modem for 500Mbps speeds?

You will need a WiFi 6 router to get the full benefit of Exetel's 500Mbps (Typical Evening Speed: 500/40 Mbps) nbn plan. WiFi 6 routers support speeds of up to 1Gbps, which means you can utilise Exetel's Warp Speed feature to enjoy 1000Mbps speeds (TES: 860/85 Mbps).

Older routers may cap your speed at 100Mbps. You can add a pre-configured high-speed modem to your Exetel order or Bring Your Own (BYO) device.

For more information, read our article on 'Different WiFi Generations'.

100 Mbps nbn plan offers download speeds up to 100Mbps. Exetel's nbn 500 is 5x faster, offering up to 500Mbps. This drastically reduces download times for large files and supports more screens at once.
